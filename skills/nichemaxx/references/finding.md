# Find the connection, then verify it

Describe what the reference needs to do in concrete terms. Example: “an ordinary sunny room feels slightly staged or untrustworthy” is a useful search; “dark luxury aesthetic” collapses the request into a genre.

Search a small set of candidates. Start with 2–3 targeted queries or a relevant archive lookup; broaden only when results are weak. Try one neighboring medium: film light through painting, interface pacing through an exhibition, fashion silhouette through furniture, or musical repetition through editing. This is a search technique, not an obligation to produce a clever analogy.

Compare candidates on fit to the present request, the freshness of the connection, factual confidence and whether a detail can actually be used. Don't produce numerical scores. Drop a candidate that matches the mood but contributes nothing specific. A familiar work with the right overlooked detail can beat an obscure weak match.

Prefer artist, publisher, label, museum, collection, film distributor or official archive pages for identity and accessible samples. Distinguish what the page documents from your interpretation of an image, recording or scene. Inspect audiovisual material before giving precise timing or editing claims; do not present a still or synopsis as having watched the work. For casual music discovery, a credible artist, label or specialist description can support a clearly attributed sonic description when playback is unavailable. Genre tags alone do not establish energy or a particular sound. Never imply you listened when you did not; mention a material limitation briefly. Verify a timestamp against the particular edition, or describe a recognizable moment without one. Don't claim reuse rights from a work's age or appearance on an archive.

Output a source, not a trail of searches. Each recommendation should survive: “What exactly am I looking at, what detail matters, and how does this help my piece?” A reference's purpose can be to contrast, loosen a habit, reveal a mechanism or provide a direction; it need not be copied visually.
